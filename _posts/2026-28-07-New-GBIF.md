**GBIF launches new website consolidating developments around taxonomic backbone handling, event data exploration, improved search and download, 
web stack consistency and custom data dashboards**

GBIF has launched a new version of the main [GBIF.org](https://www.gbif.org/) website, introducing the [Catalogue of Life](https://www.catalogueoflife.org/) as the default taxonomy for organizing occurrence data, user-customizable dashboards, 
improved presentation of sampling event data and full alignment with the technological stack powering the GBIF [hosted portals programme](https://www.gbif.org/hosted-portals). While adding new features and improved functionality, the overall site maintains a similar structure
but with many adjustments intended to improve the user experience.

With the significant [enhancements](https://www.gbif.org/news/4NYN9w8HdlGrD2mRqdvw9o/catalogue-of-life-enhancement-supports-improved-global-taxonomic-reference-for-gbif) in sources and assembly process, the Catalogue of Life (COL) becomes the default taxonomic backbone 
GBIF, against which occurrences are organized. Users will benefit from a professionally maintained global taxonomy with monthly updates 
and integration of recently described organisms. COL also enables a more nuanced taxonomy, breaking the boundaries of principal Linnean ranking. 

Since the final quarter of 2025, users have been able to explore GBIF-mediated species occurrences organized by COL and the
former taxonomic backbone in parallel. To help users in the transition, the previous GBIF website with occurrences organized 
to the old backbone will remain temporarily available at https://old.gbif.org following the launch of the new site.

**Improved exploration of event-base**

The Humboldt extension widened the scope of sampling-event datasets, enabling the indexing and searching of elements critical to 
ecological inventories. The new GBIF.org improves the findability and usability of sampling-event datasets by adding cataloging 
functionality, allowing users to identify datasets containing specific extensions, such as the Humboldt extension or Relevé.

**Data dashboards**

The new GBIF.org offers the ability to curate customized dashboards (example) for any subset of occurrence data, allowing for highly flexible and dynamic reporting across numerous facets. 

**Feedback**

Provide feedback on the website through the integrated feedback system (accessed via the rectangular speech bubble in top right corner), via Github or by email to helpdesk@gbif.org.
